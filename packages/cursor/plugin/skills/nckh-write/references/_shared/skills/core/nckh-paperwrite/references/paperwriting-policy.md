# Authoring from an evidence brief

For an outline, map the research question, claim, evidence ID and limitation to
the intended section. A proposal reports intended work and hypotheses; a thesis
or report follows its actual purpose. Unfilled results stay explicit placeholders.

For a section or argument, separate source statements, measured observations,
verified calculations/runs, interpretation and counterevidence. Keep a reader
locator for each factual claim. A resource sample gives language or reporting
advice and does not support an unrelated factual claim. Preserve citation identity.

For reporting, select the relevant domain/study design and exact venue/year/track/
article type. Clinical guidance applies only to its declared clinical scope.
Reporting completeness does not certify scientific validity.

For a revision-response, tie each response to the actual comment, changed passage,
evidence and limitations. Do not claim an experiment, analysis, reviewer approval
or author action that has not occurred. An unresolved request remains pending.

For all actions preserve the user's decisions, factual slots, quotes, glossary and
protected text. Record before/after deltas. New claims or stronger certainty need
the evidence owner; fluency, outline completeness and hashes do not close that gate.
